# The Lighthouse Keeper

The island had no name on any chart, which suited Elias fine. For eleven
years he had kept the light burning, and in all that time only three ships
had ever anchored in the cove below.

The lamp room smelled of brass polish and kerosene. Every evening at dusk
he climbed the ninety-seven steps, wound the clockwork, and watched the
beam sweep out across the water like a slow, patient hand.

# The Storm

The barometer began falling on a Tuesday. By nightfall the wind was
tearing spray off the wave tops, and Elias could feel the tower sway —
just slightly, the way it always did when the sea meant business.

He was halfway through his second pot of tea when he saw it: a light,
low on the water, where no light should be. It blinked three times,
went dark, then blinked three times again.

Someone out there was signaling.

# The Answer

Elias did the only thing a keeper could do. He answered.

Three long pulls on the shutter lever — dark, light, dark. Then he pulled
on his oilskins, took the coil of rope from the store room, and started
down the ninety-seven steps toward the sea.
